Lesson 1

What is the revenue cycle?

Revenue cycle management (RCM) connects the administrative and financial steps that help a healthcare organization get paid for services delivered.

01 · Before careRegistration, coverage, authorizations when applicable
02 · During careClinical record, services, orders and documentation
03 · After careCharge capture, coding, claim submission
04 · ResolutionAdjudication, payment, denials, patient balance

Why it matters: a problem at one step can create rework later. Strong RCM is about connecting evidence, decisions, and follow-through.

A claim is denied because required information was missing. Where should the team begin?

Not yet.A denial is not enough evidence to justify a write-off.
Good reasoning.Understand the reason, verify the record and workflow, then correct or appeal when supported by the rules.
That skips the investigation.Patient responsibility should be determined from the account facts, payer response and applicable rules.

Lesson 2

Follow the claim, not just the task.

A claim is a message asking a payer to adjudicate a service. Submission is one event; it does not mean the work is finished.

PrepareValidate patient, coverage, coding and required information.
TransmitSend through the approved clearinghouse or payer route.
ReadReview acknowledgments, status and remittance details.
ResolvePost, reconcile, correct, appeal or follow up as appropriate.

A claim has a submission acknowledgment. What does that prove?

Correct.Receipt or acceptance at one stage is different from final adjudication and payment.
Not necessarily.The claim may still be rejected, denied, pended or adjusted.
No.Patient responsibility is determined through adjudication and applicable benefits, not the submission acknowledgment alone.
Source starting points: CMS electronic billing and CMS coding and billing resources. Always check the current source and payer-specific requirements.
