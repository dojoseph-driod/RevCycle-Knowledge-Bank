Mental Health RCM: the evidence-first workflow

Think in layers: coverage status, benefit details, medical-necessity criteria when applicable, provider/network rules, authorization, documentation, claim coding and adjudication. A validation checklist should show which layer has been checked and what evidence supports it.

UnderstandIdentify the service and setting.
VerifyCheck the requirements that apply.
DocumentMake the evidence trail visible.
ResolveChoose an action supported by facts.

A member appears eligible. Can you conclude that a specific therapy visit will be paid?

Not supported.Do not assume the pathway or requirements without checking the service facts.
Correct reasoning.Start with the record, authoritative guidance and the payer-specific requirements that apply.
Avoid shortcut decisions.The route should reflect the service and rules—not convenience.

Lesson 2

Keep documentation, authorization and adjudication separate.

These checks answer different questions: what service was provided, whether prior approval was required/obtained, and how the payer processed the claim.

DocumentationWhat service and clinical facts are recorded?
AuthorizationWas it required, and was the process followed?
Claim reviewAre coding and billing details supported?
AdjudicationWhat does the remittance actually say?

A service was authorized, but the claim is denied for a different reason. What should the team conclude?

Not necessarily.Authorization is not a universal payment guarantee.
Correct.Use the denial reason and evidence to select the right follow-up.
No.The denial still needs to be reviewed and resolved through the applicable process.

Reference starting point: Official coverage / guidance resource. Use the current source and confirm jurisdiction, plan and service details before applying it operationally.
